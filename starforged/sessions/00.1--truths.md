We open with the camera flying over a large blue-white spiral galaxy, before zooming in on
a small reddish cluster a distance above the galactic plane. The perspective dives into
the cluster, and through multicoloured bands of stardust a point of light grows sharper as 
the layers of nebula peel away.

Welcome to globular cluster NSC 8412: 'The Forge'. A cluster of nebulae and isolated stars 1,700
light years above the galactic plane. It is a chaotic galaxy wracked by fiery stellar storms and 
unpredictable gravitational currents. The planets and stars of this place are forged of that chaos.

In the wake of a great cataclysm two centuries ago, our people fled the galaxy below to make a new
home here. We carved out new settlements on alien worlds, but they rarely offer a safe haven.
Conflicts rage between people and factions. Pirates prowl the spaceways in search of easy prey.
Baneful creatures and dark forces dwell on corrupted planets and in the abyssal void between stars.
We were not the first to try and tame this place, the remnants and tombs of ancient civilisations
leave a legacy of dangerous knowledge and forbidden power.

```truth
title: Cataclysm
choice: '01-33'
result: The Sun Plague extinguished the stars in our home galaxy
description:  
  The anomaly traveled at incredible speeds, many times faster than light itself,
  and snuffed out the stars around us before we realized it was coming. Few of us
  survived as we made our way to this new galaxy. Here in the Forge, the stars are
  still aflame. We cling to their warmth like weary travelers huddled around a fire.
followUp:
  title: 'We suspect the Sun Plague was caused by:'
  roll: 68
  result: A superweapon run amok
questStarter: |
  The galaxy your people left behind is a cold, lightless grave. But a solitary star
  still glows, a beacon in a vast darkness. How did this star survive the plague?
  Why do you vow to find the means to travel across the immeasurable gulf to this
  distant light?
```

The survivors never found out who pulled the trigger, but someone, somewhere did. A catalyst that 
accelerated stars' fusion to iron in days. What's worse it attached itself to the Eidolon drives
^[These are the faster-than-light (FTL) drives in Starforged. These work similar to Battlestar 
Galactica jump drives, in that they can do short bursts of FTL travel, then need time to power up. 
In Starforged this is the narrative reason for journeys being a series of steps between waypoints.]. 
Those that fled the destruction only spread it further. The bigger suns went nova, others just
collapsed into cooling ball of metal.

> Because Starforged is a reskinning of an iron age game, the importance of iron is still in its
> bones, e.g. the Swear an Iron Vow move. Reading the options here stirs up something from the 
> depth of my childhood physics understanding of massive stars' progression, fusing heavier and 
> heavier elements until they hit iron which doesn't produce enough energy to sustain further 
> progression. This wild interpretation of that will make anyone with understanding of solar 
> physics despair, but it doesn't make less sense than the concept of a faster-than-light Sun 
> Plague.

```truth
questStarter: |
  An explorer brings news. They’ve located an active gate in the depths of the Forge. Why do you
```

The stars' molten cores were the missing piece to the workings of the Iron Gates. There are tales 
still told of that hellish passage. Of ships torn asunder by rifts in the unstable wormholes, of
twisted horrors that lurked in the void between realities and slithered through the cracks we'd 
created. From a galaxy of civilisations, a few thousand ships made it through. From this far 
vantage point that disc of light still stretches out, and in a millennia or two, our children will
watch as the lights go out once more.

Only a few in the old galaxy had the resources and means to move large numbers of people through
the Iron Gates, and they used this as leverage to impose punishing terms on the survivors. With 
private armies and enforcers they have built a seat of power in the Terminus. The handful of 
free ships who made the jump, and those who have since escaped corporate control are now scattered
throughout the outlands and even the expanse. Some of these are lawless hives of scum and villainy,
others organised self-sufficient communities. Yet, even here there are corporate eyes and ears
making sure none can rival their dominance.

```truth
title: Iron
choice: '34-67'
result: Iron vows are sworn upon totems crafted from the enigmatic metal we call black iron
description: |
  Black iron was first forged by a long-dead civilization. Some say it is a living metal, attuned
  to the hidden depths of the universe. Remnants of this prized resource are found within ancient
  sites throughout the Forge. It is resistant to damage and corrosion, but can be molded using
  superheated plasma at specialized facilities. The Ironsworn carry weapons, armor, or tokens
  crafted from black iron, and swear vows upon it.
questStarter: |
  A black iron token of special significance has been stolen. What power or authority is bound to 
  this object? Who has taken it?
```

Most of the star-iron we could collect was used to activate the gates, or to purge our ships of 
the sun plague. The remnants we had were used to forge tokens of recognition for those who 
contributed most to the survival of our people. A few of those intrepid pioneers who set out to 
explore the Forge beyond the Terminus found ancient sites and artefacts, many of which made from
this same black iron. It came to be a symbol of our survival against the odds, and hope for the
future. An oath sworn on one of these tokens or artefacts has great significance to those of the 
Forge.

```truth
title: Laws
custom: true,
result: | 
  Laws and governance vary across settled domains, black iron tokens hold weight everywhere.
description: |
  The corporations have complex contract law that binds all who live in corporate space. Outside 
  their influence, each community enforces (or doesn't) their own code of laws. Across 
  jurisdictions, those Oathsworn to black iron have wide latitude to mete out justice in the 
  name of their oaths.
questStarter: |
  A former Oathsworn is on the run in the Expanse. What is your relationship to them? Why did 
  they forsake their oath? Do swear to track them down to punish them or redeem them?
```

To live in the Terminus or corporate outposts is to sign your life away into a web of complex 
contracts. Corporate controlled courts and security forces ensure obedience. Complex agreements 
of mutual support keep some checks on corporate power - at least publicly. In the outlands and 
beyond there are places where anything goes, those with constitutional laws based on the 
consent of those beholden to them, and may other systems.

Across most jurisdictions those who swear on black iron gain some remit to mete out justice in the 
name of their oaths. Bound by the weight of tradition, and the watchful eyes of other Oathsworn, 
most authorities will grant them wide latitude, as long as their oaths align with local customs.  

> This is mostly a reskinning of the middle option: varied governance with extrajudicial bounty 
> hunters. I modified it because I didn't want to have bounty hunters that were above the law. It
> felt like a cop-out to what should be a messy and complex system of relationships. I found 
> this really hard to word, the nuance of they have authority, but here are the things that 
> keep them in check was difficult to phrase succinctly. I'm still not sure that I've written this 
> well, but hopefully I can explore and define how it actually works through play.

```truth
title: Religion
roll: 88
result: | 
  Three dominant religious orders, the Triumvirate, battle for influence and power within the Forge
description: |
  Our communities are often sworn to serve one of the three doctrines of the Triumvirate. For many,
  faith offers purpose and meaning. But it also divides us. Throughout our brief history in the 
  Forge, the leaders of the Triumvirate have pitted us against one another. For this reason, some 
  are apostates who disavow these religions and follow a different path.
questStarter: |
  Quest Starter: You bear the mark of one of the gods of the Triumvirate. What is it? Priests 
  declare this as a sign you are chosen to fulfill a destiny. Do you accept this fate, and swear to
  see it through, or are you determined to see it undone? What force opposes you?
```

We know of three great divine forces:

* _Terra_ - the Goddess of Earth, she who nurtures, she who grounds. Her disciples teach of 
  considered wisdom, and cleaving to the traditions and customs of old, and passing down knowledge 
  through the generations.

* _Solus_ - the God of the Sun, the ever-burning flame, the guiding star. His followers teach of 
  ambition and following your heart. Burning brightly so that your legacy will live on in the 
  tales of your deeds.

* _Tempest_ - the Wrath of the Storm, the primordial chaos, the ever-changing. Their faithful teach 
  of adaptability, of preparedness, and decisiveness in the face of adversity.

Not all worship these, many respect all three to varying degrees, and each has their zealots and 
quiet adherents as well. 

It is claimed that black iron is their combined work: Birthed from the womb of Terra, the fires of 
Solus shape its form, and the spark of Tempest gives it life.

> I went back and forth on this. I rolled this option, because I thought I didn't care which option 
> came up. Then the wide-ranging implications of the prompt's conflict and the rigidity and galactic
> nature of the faiths made me reject it. I switched to the middle option of diverse faiths, but in 
> writing up my decision, I persuaded myself that this version met the spirit of the original 
> prompt, and switched back. I have left these deliberately vague to define them more through play 
> as characters explore their faith (or lack thereof.)

```truth
questStarter: |
  A reactor exploded at a remote settlement, killing several and exposing many others to lethal
```

The corporations have very advanced medical care... for those who are privileged enough to receive 
it. This was one thing they could not keep to themselves. Over the generations all manor of 
health workers have defected to join the Terran Apothecary. These healers travel through the Forge, 
tending to those that need it, and have cells embedded through-out the Terminus. The Corporations' 
attempts to move against them have been blunted by popular support.

```truth
title: Artificial Intelligence
custom: true
result: True machine intelligence is not possible without life.
description: |
  Information technology and machine intelligence is roughly on par with current technology. The
  corporations have access to vast computing power at scale, but it is not intelligent. There 
  have been secretive experiments into cybernetic person-machine hybrids. Some archaeologists 
  theorise that the progenitors managed to work the spark of intelligence into black iron devices.
questStarter: |
  You hear news of an old acquaintance who has fallen foul of corporate law, and imprisoned in a 
  deep space black site. Why do you swear to break them out? What experiments have they been

```

The devotees of Tempest would have you believe there is some divine spark to intelligence.
Certainly research into pure machine intelligence have always hit against physical limitations that 
could not be overcome. 

There are rumours that some corporations have successfully combined biological and artificial 
components together. There is a sect of Tempest followers who delve progenitor tombs who are 
convinced they have evidence of intelligent black iron devices. They site holy scripture that 
claims intelligence comes from Tempest's spark, and it is this that separates black iron from 
base metals.

```truth
title: War
custom: true
result: 'Advanced weaponry is concentrated in the corporations, engaged in a perpetual cold war'
description: |
  Most of the advanced weaponry brought over in the Exodus and developed since are in the hands 
  of the corporations. Outlands settlements defend their own with cruder tech, and what they can 
  smuggle out of corporate control. Any that try to throw their weight enough to come to the 
  attention of corporate agents are swiftly put down.
```

The corporations have a tight control over most weaponry, most civilian ships limited to what 
can be legitimately argued to be for destroying cosmic debris, or self-defence against raiders 
or the horrors of the cosmos. 

That is not to say there are no wars. Settlements that have tried to stockpile weaponry have 
been annihilated when they come to the attention of the nearest corporation. In some conflicts 
different corporations have armed both sides to fight a proxy war. In others, they are conducted 
through stealth raids and espionage, with the antagonists trying to keep under the corporations' 
radar.

> Whilst there will be some threats that want to mete out violence on our protagonists, I want 
> to keep combat from being a dominant mechanic in this game. The corporations should be too 
> overwhelming to defeat through force, and other's should have reason to choose non-violent 
> resolutions for conflicts.

```truth
title: Horrors
roll: 76
result: The strange energies of the Forge give unnatural life to the dead.
description: |
  The woken dead are a plague within the Forge. Some of these beings are benevolent or seek 
  absolution, but most are hollowed and corrupted by death. They are driven by hate and a hunger 
  for the warmth of life that is forever lost to them. The Soulbinders are dedicated to putting
```

Whether some quirk of the energies here, a curse from the Iron Gates, or something else, the 
dead do not go quietly into Terra's cold embrace. Those that can not be buried deep in planetary 
earth will rise to plague the living. The Soulbinders are a sect of Solus faithful who claim to use 
divine light to purge the restless dead. Divine or not, their rites and icons seem to do the job. 
There are rumours of corporate necromancers who use the secrets of black iron to bind the dead to 
their service.
